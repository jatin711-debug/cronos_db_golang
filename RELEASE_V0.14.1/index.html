    CronosDB v0.14.1¶

    This release takes costs off a leader's publish, acknowledgement and read paths. In the soak at 450 publishes per second the nodes that lead partitions use about a fifth of a core where v0.14.0 used a third to two fifths. A partition with sixteen publishers takes four times as many publishes per second, a group with a small credit window is delivered three times as many events or more, and forty idle client connections hold 4 MiB of memory instead of 161. It changes no API, flag, wire message or on-disk format: v0.14.0 data directories and cluster state are used as they are, and v0.14.0 reads what v0.14.1 has written. It is not 1.0: the release gates of the 1.0 readiness review are still open (a passing 24-hour soak, a soak on separate hosts, power-loss testing on real disks, recovery and upgrade drills at production size).

    Who should upgrade¶

    Clustered deployments with more than one publisher on a partition, with several consumers on a partition, or with consumer groups whose credit window is small: those are where a partition took one publish, or one stream's acknowledgements, per disk sync. Also nodes with many client connections and little memory, and encrypted deployments with change feeds or consumers that follow the end of the log, where reads cost a leader the most CPU.

    The supported upgrade from v0.14.0 is unchanged: stop every node, replace the binary or image, and start them again; data directories are kept. Nothing is converted at the first start, and going back to v0.14.0 is the same procedure.

    What behaves differently¶

    • every_event shares syncs on a replicated partition. A publish is still acknowledged only after a sync that began after its append, on the leader and on enough followers. Publishes that arrive while one sync runs now share the next one, as they already did with batch; before, the partition took one publish per sync.
    • A leader sends a publish to its followers while its own sync runs. A follower can therefore hold an entry durably before its leader does. The leader counts itself among the replicas that hold an entry only as far as its own log is durable, so an entry is acknowledged to its publisher, handed to consumers and exported no earlier than before: when enough replicas, the leader among them as far as it has synced, hold it durably.
    • Credits come back when an acknowledgement is read, not when its completion is on disk. The AckResponse is still sent only once the completion is durable, and the events of a delivery are not handed out again in between. A node that loses power between the two delivers both windows again; before, it delivered one.
    • A connection's buffers are 64 KiB, not 4 MiB, for reading and for writing. Publishing batches of about 4 MB over loopback without TLS took 8.0 ms instead of 7.4; batches of 400 KB took the same. Behind TLS a read returns one record of at most 16 KiB whatever the buffer.
    • New log segments get an index entry at least every 256 KiB, as well as every --index-interval events. An entry is 24 bytes held in memory, at most 10 MiB for 100 GiB of log. Segments written before keep the index they have, so reads of old segments cost what they did.

    Changes¶

    • Publishes to one partition share the leader's sync, and the leader's disk works while the followers' do. A replicated publish appended under the partition's publish lock and waited there for its sync, and the followers were sent an event only once the leader's disk had finished with it. The append now writes its records out and gives the lock back; the publish waits for its sync and for its followers outside it. A leader and two followers on one disk, Linux, every_event: one publisher, 11 to 17 ms a publish before and 5.3 ms now.
    • A publish is told it is replicated when its follower says so. A send to a follower carries the batches queued behind it. The sends of those batches still took whatever had been queued since and sent that before they finished, so with sixteen publishers on a partition a publish waited about ten round trips for an answer its follower had given in the first. With the change above: 187 to 245 publishes a second before, 962 now, and a request to a follower carries 8 events instead of 1.7.
    • The acknowledgements of every stream that waits are recorded in one write. Each consumer stream brought its acknowledgements in a call of its own, and each call made its own durable write, one after the other. Calls that arrive during a write now wait and are recorded together by the next. Sixteen consumers acknowledging four events at a time on one partition: 561 batches a second before, 3,346 now.
    • A consumer's next window does not wait for the disk. See "Credits come back" above. One node, a group with a window of 64: 5,100 to 17,900 events a second from publish to acknowledgement before, 55,600 now; from a backlog, 22,200 to 23,300 before and 71,200 now. With a window of 2,000 nothing changes (about 76,000 and 235,000).
    • A read finds its first record without decrypting the ones before it. A range read found its first record from the nearest index entry by reading, and with encryption decrypting, every record in between: five hundred on average for a reader following the end of the log. It now passes over them by their lengths, which are stored in the clear, as a point read already did, and the denser index leaves fewer to pass over. On an encrypted log, reading the newest event took 1.1 ms and takes 22 µs; reading one event anywhere took 234 µs and takes 42 µs. In the v0.13.1 soak these reads were 15 to 23 percent of a leader's CPU for the change feed and about a tenth for events the scheduler brought back from disk.
    • The progress journal drops its oldest entries without moving the rest. A partition keeps its recent completions, up to 262,144 offsets, for followers that need only what changed. Once it held that many, every acknowledgement copied all of them to drop the oldest: 22 to 24 percent of a leader's CPU in the v0.13.1 soak. An acknowledgement recorded in a full journal took 1.2 ms and takes 147 ns.
    • Idle connections hold little memory. Both gRPC servers gave each connection a 4 MiB read buffer and a 4 MiB write buffer for as long as it lasted: forty idle client connections held 161 MiB of heap, and hold 3.7 MiB now.

    The publish, acknowledgement and delivery figures are from Linux (WSL2, ext4) and the read, journal and memory figures from Windows, each with the benchmark named in its commit.

    Compatibility¶

    • On disk: nothing changes format. Index files of new segments hold more entries in the same format, which v0.14.0 reads.
    • Wire: unchanged. v0.14.0 and v0.14.1 nodes work together during an upgrade: a v0.14.1 leader sends to its followers earlier than a v0.14.0 leader does, and followers of either version answer as before.
    • Flags: none added, removed or changed. --index-interval still gives the spacing in events; the spacing in bytes is fixed.
    • Clients: unchanged. A consumer with a small credit window is sent its next window sooner.

    Verification¶

    Before the release:

    • CI (run 44997241670): every job passed, including the race suite, the fault campaign and the container tests.
    • Nightly jobs (run 44997241289): the acceptance suite passed three times in a row and the container suite once; the rolling upgrade, rollback and upgrade again passed, starting from v0.13.1, not from v0.14.0 as this page first said (a run started by hand took the default release of the catch-up comparison; the workflow now starts from the last release, and the upgrade from v0.14.0 was tested after the release, below); TestPowerLoss passed on a GitHub runner with every_event and batch; and the soak's remote mode passed against three nodes driven by command. The run before it (44899803697) failed in one test, TestFollowerFarBehindCatchesUpFromASnapshot, on the third pass of the suite: the test waited for a snapshot copy, and the leaders had dropped a finished segment 40 ms before the node returned, which left it fewer entries behind than the threshold, so it was caught up by replay, as it should be. The test now has its leaders keep their logs.
    • Soak comparison with v0.14.0 (nightly workflow, catchup_ab with profiles, run 44899806945): 40 minutes at 450 publishes per second with each server, three nodes of 2 CPUs and 384 MiB on one GitHub runner. Both passed, with nothing refused, early or of unknown outcome.

    Known limits¶

    Unchanged from v0.14.0, and this release makes no new capacity claim. The soaks ran on one host, so the separate-host soak gate stays open.

    Measured for the first time in this release's soak, and also in v0.14.0: when a partition's leadership moves, its new leader can deliver again events that its consumer groups had already finished. Nothing is lost or skipped, and consumers must handle repeats in any case, but the numbers are larger than a change of leader should cost.

    • A node that returns is handed its partitions back before it knows what was finished while it was away. The handoff waits for the node's log to end where the leader's does. The record of what each group has finished is copied to it separately, a page of 4,096 completions every half second, and the handoff does not wait for that. In the soak a leader was away for 286 seconds at 450 publishes per second; 12 seconds after it returned it led its two partitions again and delivered about 64,000 events to each group a second time, which is what was appended while it was away. The nightly soak, with a 30-second outage at a tenth of the rate, repeats about 880 events per group on v0.14.0, and 800 and 220 on this release.
    • Later in a long run, a follower that takes over repeats more. The first failover of the soak, 20 minutes in, repeated nothing. One 77 minutes in, to a node that had never been away, repeated 15,920 events for one group and 1,945 for the other, and the restart of the whole cluster after three hours repeated 35,313 and 68,115. The followers' copies of the groups' progress were behind by then; why is not established yet. The code that copies them is the same in v0.14.0.

    Both are the next release's work.

    Found while testing this release, and also in v0.14.0: a node that already knows its partitions' leaders when it starts asks each for a copy of its log and then refuses to install it, because the check of the partition's log format is not in place that early in the start. Its log shows Failed to sync partition N: ... does not require the term-start format. The node's own log is untouched and it catches up as usual, but the start waits for the download, and the downloaded files stay in the partition's snapshot-staging directory. Most starts do not know the leaders that early and skip the copy. The next release removes it.
